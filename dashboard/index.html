40 watchlist protocols, ranked by implied value

The watchlist is the floor, never the ceiling: 40 pre-token protocols at or above $50M TVL, plus the 158 strongest pre-token protocols the watchlist does not name. Each implied value is priced off a curve fitted to the snapshot's own live tokens — market cap grows sublinearly with TVL, so the giants price near $50–115M, not billions — then adjusted by category, seasoning and momentum. Every row carries the band the launch could land in and a farm verdict for a $1,000 stake. Never a promise that a token launches, or what it will be worth if it does.

How the model works (radar-5)

Implied value = curve value × category factor × seasoning × momentum × chain. Every number comes from this snapshot — no protocol name is ever special-cased.

The curve the board is priced off

  • Curve value. log10(mcap) = a + b × log10(tvl), fitted by least squares on the snapshot's live tokens (exchanges and bridges excluded — their tokens price off custody and volume, not DeFi TVL). The fit is reported honestly: R² is only 0.13 and barely half the comparables land within 3× of the curve, so the curve is a starting point, not a verdict — which is why the category factor and the residual band are shown.
  • Category factor. Each category's median residual — its live tokens' actual mcap divided by the curve's prediction — shrunk toward the global median residual by peer count (weight n ÷ (n + 5)) and capped at 3.00×. A category with one peer cannot set the price of the board: Risk Curators' single peer sits 7.01× above the curve, but the factor is 1.99×; Prediction Market's 682× residual is capped at 3.00×. A category with no live peers takes a factor of exactly 1: the curve itself.
  • Seasoning. Near-flat by design: the live data shows no age premium — protocols 1–2 years old price no higher per TVL than 3–5-year-olds — so seasoning moves value by at most ±3%.
  • Momentum. The protocol's 7-day TVL change relative to its category's median change (clamped to −60%…+120% first): 1 + 0.50 × (change − category median) ÷ 100, clamped to 0.85…1.20. The live data shows only a weak positive tilt, so the clamp is tight.
  • Leave-one-out validation. R² is only 0.13 — TVL alone explains little of a token's price — so the page does not rest its case on the fit. Refit the curve 101 times, each time without one comparable, and the median price lands within ×3.13 of the truth; a flat median multiple lands within ×5.06. The curve beats the baseline out of sample, which is the claim that matters. The same residuals give every row its band: half the comparables land between 0.33× and 2.91× of the curve, so a row's range is its implied value times 0.33× to 2.96× (quartiles divided by the median residual).
  • Range. Every row shows the band the launch could land in, under its implied value and in its detail view: the middle half of the comparables' residuals around the curve, applied to that row's value. A point estimate on a model this noisy would be false precision.
  • Farm verdict. Every row is judged for a $1,000 stake: the slice it holds, minus the gas the plan costs to run. Worth at +$10 or more, thin from $0, skip below $0. A big-TVL row on an expensive chain can read skip even with a large implied value — the slice is what a farmer takes home, and the gas is what it costs to get there.
  • Chain. Exactly 1.00 in radar-5. Multi-chain live tokens are worth no more per TVL than single-chain ones (median residual 1.02 for 5+ chains vs 1.28 for one chain), so chain diversity earns no premium. The sandbox can still test a weight above zero.
  • Slice per $1K. What a $1,000 farm stake holds at the radar's price, if 10% of the launch reaches depositors: $1,000 × implied ÷ TVL × 0.10. The farm plan of every row states the same figure for its own capital. Gas figures are typical estimates per chain, not quotes.
